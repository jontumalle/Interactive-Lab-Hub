# Paper Prototype Feedback

Reported by Jonathan on November 18 2026.

## Reported outcome

Jonathan reported that the paper experiment worked and received positive feedback that the experience made sense. The main feedback was that the presentation of the essential clues will be important in the final design. This feedback concerned clues, not optional hints.

This is early evidence that the paper experience is understandable. It does not establish hardware reliability or how the experience will work with the final enclosure and screen. Participant count, completion time, hint use, test date, and detailed observations have not yet been recorded here.

## Design response

Keep the current puzzle logic for the first hardware iteration. Develop the presentation of each essential clue alongside its associated physical interaction:

| Essential information | Proposed presentation to test |
| --- | --- |
| Two diagnostic records | Separate readable maintenance records that players can discover and compare beside the wheel. Keep both available after discovery. |
| Recovered encrypted dispatch | A clear receiver display with distinct source labels and symbols; allow replay or review while decoding. |
| Attribute legend | A fixed reference beside the wheel, readable without covering or moving the disk. |
| Transmitter port specifications | A readable register beside the patch panel, visible while connecting cables. |

These presentation choices are proposed responses to the feedback, not findings already demonstrated by the experiment. Optional hints remain separately requested assistance; they are not how players obtain essential clues.

## Next check

Build and test the waveform interaction and encoder wheel. During the rough case layout, check whether players can find, read, revisit, and combine the clues while operating the hardware. Capture observations, photos, and the changes made after testing.
