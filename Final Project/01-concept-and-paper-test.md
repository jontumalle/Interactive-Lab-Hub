## Updated big idea

Create a portable escape room in a briefcase. Players decode a secret message by solving three connected puzzles that combine physical mechanisms and electronic controls such as a joystick or encoder. Selected puzzle components and mounting panels will be fabricated with a laser cutter. Each solution reveals information or access needed for the next puzzle, leading to the final message. The experience should be understandable to new players and resettable for another group.

## Paper-test procedure

1. Prepare the opening instructions, three sets of clues, a final message, and hint cards. Use cardboard stand-ins for moving pieces where manipulation is necessary to understand the puzzle.
2. Arrange them in a full-size outline of the proposed case. Label dimensions as provisional until an actual case is measured.
3. Recruit two people who have not been taught the solutions. Record whether they play together or separately. Explain the goal without explaining how to solve the puzzles.
4. Manually trigger reveals only when the corresponding solution is reached. State simulated feedback consistently; do not silently fix confusing clues during a run.
5. Record time, attempts, requests for help, and where progress stops. Distinguish difficulty interpreting a clue from difficulty operating the physical object.
6. Ask players what they thought each control did, which transitions made sense, and what felt confusing. Reset the prototype and note anything difficult to restore.
7. Revise at least one observed problem and record the reason. If possible, check the revision with a fresh player.

## Updated fallback

If custom fabrication or moving mechanisms prove unreliable, keep all three puzzles using cardboard or simple flat panels inside an existing briefcase. Replace automated releases with envelopes or combination locks while retaining the electronic interactions that have already been validated.
